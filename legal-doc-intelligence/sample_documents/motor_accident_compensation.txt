The Judgment of the Court was delivered by NAIR, J.

This appeal arises out of a claim for compensation under the Motor Vehicles
Act. The claimants are the widow and two minor children of the deceased, who
died in a road accident caused by a lorry belonging to the first respondent
and insured by the second respondent.

The deceased, aged thirty-four, was employed as a schoolteacher earning
Rs. 22,000 per month. The Motor Accident Claims Tribunal awarded compensation
of Rs. 18,00,000. The claimants appeal for enhancement, and the insurer has
filed a cross-appeal disputing negligence.

Learned counsel for the appellant claimants contended that the Tribunal
applied too low a multiplier and failed to add amounts for future prospects,
loss of consortium and loss of the children's care. He submitted that the
income should have been increased to account for likely career progression.

Counsel for the respondent insurer submitted that the lorry driver was not
solely responsible, as the deceased was riding a two-wheeler without a helmet
and had contributed to the accident. He urged that any enhancement should be
reduced for contributory negligence.

We have considered the evidence. In our opinion, the eye-witness account and
the site plan establish that the lorry was on the wrong side of the road, and
the absence of a helmet did not contribute to a collision of this nature. The
Court holds that the proper multiplier for the deceased's age is sixteen, and
that an addition of forty per cent for future prospects is warranted, together
with conventional heads of loss.

The award is enhanced to Rs. 42,00,000 with interest at seven and a half per
cent per annum from the date of the claim petition. The claimants' appeal is
allowed and the insurer's cross-appeal is dismissed.
