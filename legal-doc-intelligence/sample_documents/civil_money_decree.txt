The Judgment of the Court was delivered by MEHTA, J.

The appellant is a construction company that entered into a contract with the
respondent for the supply of steel reinforcement bars. The dispute concerns
the respondent's failure to deliver the agreed quantity within the stipulated
period, which the appellant claims caused delay and additional expenditure on
its building project.

The appellant filed a suit before the Civil Court seeking damages of
Rs. 18,00,000 for breach of contract. The trial court decreed the suit in
part, awarding Rs. 7,50,000. On appeal, the first appellate court dismissed
the claim entirely, holding that the appellant had failed to prove actual
loss. The present appeal arises from that dismissal.

Learned counsel for the appellant contended that the respondent had admitted
the delay in its written statement, and that the correspondence on record
established the additional cost incurred in procuring bars from an alternative
supplier at a higher rate. He submitted that the first appellate court had
ignored this documentary evidence.

On the other hand, counsel for the respondent submitted that the contract
contained no clause fixing time as the essence, and that the appellant had
continued to accept deliveries after the stipulated date, thereby waiving its
right to claim damages for delay.

We have considered the submissions and examined the record. In our opinion,
time was not expressly made the essence of the contract, but the respondent
does not dispute that the delay occurred and that the appellant procured
substitute material. The Court holds that the appellant is entitled to be
compensated for the proven difference in price, which the invoices place at
Rs. 5,20,000. A claim for loss of profit on the larger project, however, is
too remote and is not supported by evidence.

The judgment of the first appellate court is set aside. The respondent is
held liable and is ordered to pay the appellant a sum of Rs. 5,20,000 with
interest at six per cent per annum from the date of suit until realisation.
The appeal is allowed in part, with costs.
