The Judgment of the Court was delivered by RANGARAJAN, J.

The petitioner joined service as a Lower Division Clerk in the State
Secretariat in 1978 and was promoted to Upper Division Clerk in 1990. On
11 March 2001 the State issued an order superseding the petitioner and
promoting a junior, one Suresh Kumar, to the post of Section Officer. The
petitioner challenges that order by way of this writ petition.

The facts are not seriously in dispute. The petitioner's name stood above
that of the junior in the seniority list, and the Departmental Promotion
Committee had graded the petitioner as "good" and the junior as "very good".
The State relied on the latter grading to justify the supersession.

Learned counsel for the petitioner contended that supersession on the basis
of a single year's grading, without considering the entire service record,
was arbitrary and violated Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution. He
submitted that the petitioner's confidential reports over two decades were
uniformly favourable.

The respondent State submitted that promotion to a selection post is governed
by merit and not merely by seniority, and that the Departmental Promotion
Committee was entitled to prefer the candidate with the higher grading. It
urged that the Court should not sit in appeal over the Committee's assessment.

We have considered the rival contentions. In our opinion, while merit may
legitimately override seniority for a selection post, the assessment of merit
must rest on the whole record and not on an isolated entry. The Court observes
that the Committee confined itself to the gradings of one year and did not
advert to the petitioner's long and consistent record. Such an approach
renders the decision arbitrary.

The order of supersession dated 11 March 2001 is quashed. The State is
directed to reconvene the Departmental Promotion Committee and reconsider the
petitioner's case on the entire service record within three months. The writ
petition is allowed, with no order as to costs.
