The Judgment of the Court was delivered by SUBRAMANIAM, J.

The appellant is a private limited company engaged in the manufacture of
textiles. The appeal concerns whether a sum of Rs. 65,00,000 claimed as
expenditure on the replacement of machinery is allowable as revenue
expenditure or must be treated as capital in nature.

The Assessing Officer disallowed the claim and treated it as capital
expenditure, allowing only depreciation. The Commissioner of Income Tax
(Appeals) upheld the disallowance, but the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal
reversed it and allowed the full deduction. The Revenue is in appeal.

Learned counsel for the appellant Revenue contended that the replacement
brought into existence a new asset of enduring benefit and therefore
constituted capital expenditure. He submitted that the Tribunal had erred in
treating the entire outlay as current repairs.

Counsel for the respondent assessee submitted that the machinery replaced was
only a part of an integrated spinning plant, that it did not increase
production capacity, and that the expenditure was incurred merely to keep the
existing plant operational.

We have considered the contentions. In our opinion, the test is whether the
expenditure brings into existence a new and distinct advantage or merely
preserves an existing asset. The Court finds that the replaced components were
part of a larger integrated plant and did not enhance capacity, so the outlay
is in the nature of current repairs allowable as revenue expenditure.

The order of the Tribunal is upheld and the appeal of the Revenue is
dismissed. The deduction is allowed in full.
