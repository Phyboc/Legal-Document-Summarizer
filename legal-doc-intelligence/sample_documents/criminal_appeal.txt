The Judgment of the Court was delivered by KRISHNAN, J.

The appellant was convicted by the Sessions Court under Section 302 of the
Indian Penal Code for the murder of one Ramesh Pillai and was sentenced to
imprisonment for life. The conviction was confirmed by the High Court, and
the appellant has preferred this appeal by special leave.

The prosecution case was that on the night of 14 August 1981, following a
dispute over a boundary wall, the appellant assaulted the deceased with a
wooden stick, causing injuries from which he died two days later. The case
rested chiefly on the testimony of two eye-witnesses and a dying declaration
recorded by the investigating officer.

Learned counsel for the appellant contended that the dying declaration was
unreliable because it was not recorded by a Magistrate and the deceased was
in no fit condition to make a statement. He further submitted that the two
eye-witnesses were close relatives of the deceased and therefore interested
witnesses whose evidence required careful scrutiny.

The respondent State submitted that the dying declaration was consistent with
the medical evidence and that the relationship of the witnesses to the
deceased did not, by itself, render their testimony untrustworthy. The State
urged that the concurrent findings of the two courts below should not be
disturbed.

We have considered the arguments of both sides. In our opinion, a dying
declaration can form the sole basis of conviction only when the court is
satisfied that it is voluntary and truthful. The medical evidence shows that
the deceased had suffered a head injury and was semi-conscious when the
statement was recorded. The Court finds that it is unsafe to rely on such a
declaration without independent corroboration, and the evidence of the
interested witnesses does not supply that corroboration beyond reasonable
doubt.

The conviction and sentence are set aside. The appellant is acquitted of the
charge and is directed to be released forthwith unless required in any other
case. The appeal is allowed.
