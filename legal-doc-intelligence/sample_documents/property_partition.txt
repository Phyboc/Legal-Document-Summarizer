The Judgment of the Court was delivered by RAO, J.

The appellant filed a suit for partition claiming a one-fourth share in
ancestral property against his three brothers, the respondents. The dispute
concerns whether a prior family arrangement had already divided the property
and barred a fresh partition.

The trial court decreed the suit and granted the appellant a one-fourth share.
The first appellate court reversed the decree, holding that an oral family
settlement of 1995, acted upon for many years, had already partitioned the
property. The High Court affirmed that view, and the appellant is before this
Court.

Learned counsel for the appellant contended that an oral family settlement
affecting immovable property of this value required a registered instrument,
and that the alleged settlement was never reduced to writing or acted upon in
respect of his share.

Counsel for the respondents submitted that a family arrangement recording a
pre-existing division does not require registration, and that the parties had
mutated the lands, paid separate revenue, and enjoyed distinct portions for
over two decades.

We have considered the submissions. In our opinion, a family arrangement that
merely records and gives effect to an antecedent division of property does not
require registration, and long, separate possession is strong evidence of such
an arrangement. The Court finds that the conduct of the parties over twenty
years confirms a concluded partition.

The concurrent findings of the courts below are affirmed and the appeal is
dismissed. The suit for partition stands rejected.
