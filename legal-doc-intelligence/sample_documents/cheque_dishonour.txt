The Judgment of the Court was delivered by BANERJEE, J.

The appellant was prosecuted under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments
Act for the dishonour of a cheque for Rs. 4,00,000 issued to the respondent
towards repayment of a loan. The dispute concerns whether the statutory
presumption of a legally enforceable debt was rebutted.

The trial Magistrate convicted the appellant and sentenced him to a fine of
Rs. 8,00,000 as compensation. The Sessions Court upheld the conviction, and
the High Court dismissed the revision. The appellant has approached this Court
by special leave.

Learned counsel for the appellant contended that the cheque had been given as
a blank security cheque for an earlier transaction that was fully settled, and
that the complainant had misused it. He submitted that the appellant's bank
statement showed no corresponding loan advanced by the complainant.

The respondent complainant submitted that the issuance of the cheque and the
signature were admitted, which raised the presumption under Section 139, and
that the appellant had led no credible evidence to rebut it.

We have considered the submissions. In our opinion, once issuance is admitted
the presumption of a legally enforceable debt arises, but it is rebuttable on
a preponderance of probabilities. The Court finds that the appellant's bank
records, which show no loan credited, raise a probable defence that the
complainant failed to counter by proving his financial capacity to lend.

The conviction and sentence under Section 138 are set aside and the appellant
is acquitted. The appeal is allowed.
