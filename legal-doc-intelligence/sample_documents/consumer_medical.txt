The Judgment of the Court was delivered by PILLAI, J.

The appellant is a patient who underwent surgery at the respondent hospital
and alleges that negligence during the operation caused permanent disability.
The dispute concerns whether the hospital and the operating surgeon are liable
for medical negligence under the Consumer Protection Act.

The complainant underwent an abdominal surgery, after which a surgical sponge
was found to have been left inside and had to be removed in a second
operation. The State Commission awarded compensation of Rs. 5,00,000, which
the National Commission reduced to Rs. 2,00,000. The complainant appeals for
restoration of the original award.

Learned counsel for the appellant contended that leaving a sponge inside the
body is a clear case of negligence that speaks for itself, and that the
reduced award did not reflect the pain, second surgery and loss of earning
during a long convalescence.

Counsel for the respondent hospital submitted that the sponge count was the
responsibility of the nursing staff, that the surgeon acted with due care, and
that the compensation awarded was adequate.

We have considered the submissions. In our opinion, a retained surgical sponge
is a textbook instance where the principle of res ipsa loquitur applies, and
the hospital is vicariously liable for the acts of its staff and surgeon. The
Court finds that the National Commission gave no sound reason for reducing the
award and underestimated the suffering caused.

The compensation is restored to Rs. 5,00,000 with interest at nine per cent
per annum from the date of the complaint. The appeal is allowed.
