The Judgment of the Court was delivered by QURESHI, J.

The appellant seeks regular bail in a case registered under the Prevention of
Corruption Act, alleging that as a public servant he demanded and accepted an
illegal gratification. The dispute concerns whether the appellant is entitled
to bail pending trial.

The appellant, a revenue official, was arrested on the allegation of accepting
a bribe of Rs. 50,000 for issuing a land record. He has been in custody for
seven months. The Special Court rejected his bail application, and the High
Court declined to interfere.

Learned counsel for the appellant contended that the investigation is
complete, the charge-sheet has been filed, and the appellant, a government
servant with roots in society, poses no flight risk. He submitted that the
trial would take years and continued detention served no purpose.

The respondent State submitted that the appellant held a sensitive post, that
witnesses in the department are subordinate to him, and that his release would
enable him to tamper with evidence and influence those witnesses.

We have considered the submissions. In our opinion, with the charge-sheet
already filed the apprehension of tampering can be met by suitable conditions
rather than by continued incarceration, and the seriousness of the charge
alone is not a ground to deny bail indefinitely. The Court finds that further
pre-trial detention is not justified.

The appellant is directed to be released on bail on furnishing a personal bond
with two sureties, on condition that he shall not enter the department premises
and shall appear at every hearing. The appeal is allowed.
