The Judgment of the Court was delivered by IYER, J.

The appellants are owners of agricultural land in village Ambli that was
acquired by the State for the construction of an irrigation canal. The dispute
concerns the amount of compensation payable for the acquired land.

The Land Acquisition Officer fixed the market value at Rs. 40 per square
metre. On a reference under Section 18, the District Court enhanced it to
Rs. 95 per square metre. The State appealed against the enhancement, and the
land-owners filed cross-objections seeking a further increase.

Learned counsel for the appellant land-owners contended that the reference
court had wrongly ignored sale deeds of comparable plots in the same village
that reflected a value of over Rs. 150 per square metre. He submitted that the
acquired land had frontage on a metalled road and potential for non-
agricultural use.

The respondent State submitted that the sale deeds relied upon related to
small developed plots and could not be a safe guide for valuing a large block
of agricultural land, which must be assessed with an appropriate deduction for
development.

We have considered the material on record. In our opinion, the sale instances
of small plots are relevant but require a deduction towards the cost of
development when applied to a large agricultural holding. The Court finds that
a deduction of one-third is reasonable, which yields a market value of
Rs. 110 per square metre.

The compensation is enhanced to Rs. 110 per square metre, with all statutory
benefits including solatium and interest on the enhanced amount. The appeal of
the State is dismissed and the cross-objections are allowed in part.
