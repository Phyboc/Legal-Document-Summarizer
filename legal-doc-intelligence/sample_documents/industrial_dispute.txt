The Judgment of the Court was delivered by GHOSH, J.

The appellant workman was dismissed from service by the respondent company
after a domestic inquiry into charges of unauthorised absence and insubordination.
The dispute concerns whether the dismissal was justified and whether the
Labour Court was right in ordering reinstatement.

The workman had been in service for twelve years. The management conducted an
inquiry, found the charges proved, and dismissed him. On a reference, the
Labour Court held the inquiry to be fair but found the punishment
disproportionate and ordered reinstatement with fifty per cent back wages.

Learned counsel for the appellant workman contended that the charges were
trivial, that the absence was on account of documented illness, and that
dismissal for a first lapse in a long career was shockingly disproportionate.
He supported the Labour Court's exercise of discretion.

Counsel for the respondent company submitted that the Labour Court, having
upheld the inquiry, had no jurisdiction to interfere with the quantum of
punishment, and that insubordination struck at the root of workplace
discipline.

We have considered the rival contentions. In our opinion, where an inquiry is
fair, the Labour Court may still interfere with the punishment under Section
11A if it is disproportionate to the misconduct proved. The Court finds that
dismissal for an illness-related absence, after twelve years of unblemished
service, is excessive, though full back wages are not warranted.

The order of reinstatement is upheld with continuity of service, but the back
wages are reduced from fifty per cent to twenty-five per cent. The appeal is
allowed in part.
