The Judgment of the Court was delivered by DESAI, J.

The appellant is the landlord of a shop premises let to the respondent tenant
for carrying on a cloth business. The appellant sought eviction on the grounds
of bona fide personal requirement and default in payment of rent.

The Rent Controller ordered eviction on the ground of personal requirement,
holding that the landlord's son needed the shop to start a business. The
Appellate Authority reversed that finding, and the High Court declined to
interfere. The landlord is in appeal before this Court.

Learned counsel for the appellant landlord contended that the need of the
landlord's son was genuine and that the courts below had wrongly treated the
availability of other premises as decisive. He submitted that a landlord is
entitled to choose the premises most suitable for the intended business.

The respondent tenant submitted that the landlord owned two other vacant shops
in the same locality, which showed that the claimed requirement was not bona
fide but a device to secure higher rent from a new tenant.

We have considered the rival submissions. In our opinion, the existence of
alternative premises is a relevant circumstance but is not by itself
conclusive against the landlord. The Court observes, however, that the
landlord gave no explanation for leaving the two other shops vacant, and that
omission casts serious doubt on the genuineness of the stated need.

The order of eviction is set aside and the appeal is dismissed. The tenant
shall continue in possession on payment of the arrears of rent within two
months.
